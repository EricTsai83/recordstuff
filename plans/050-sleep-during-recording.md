# 050 — Keep awake while recording, stop and save on sleep

[English](050-sleep-during-recording.md) | [繁體中文](050-sleep-during-recording.zh-TW.md)

Status: implemented and natively checked on 2026-09-28; the notification release after waking was changed from a timer to a user-return check after the first sleep test, and was re-checked the same day ([record](../docs/verification/history-2026-09.md#plan-050-keep-awake-and-stop-on-sleep--2026-09-28)); closure awaits the maintainer. Created: 2026-09-28. The only queue item. Execution order: see [queue](README.md#order-and-status).

## Purpose and boundary

Nothing keeps the Mac awake while RecordStuff records. A long recording without input, such as a video or a presentation being captured, can reach the display-off or idle-sleep timeout. Cap's message for the same event ("Screen capture stopped because macOS made the display unavailable. This commonly happens when the lid is closed or the display sleeps.") suggests that capture then ends, which RecordStuff reports as a capture failure; this has not been observed natively here, and this plan's acceptance records it. When the user puts the Mac to sleep during a recording, 035's N31 observed it on 2026-09-27: `power: suspend`, 146 ms later `capture_failed capture source ended (display or audio track stopped)`, an 8.9 s partial kept, and the failure banner requested during suspend and never seen after waking ([record](../docs/verification/history-2026-09.md#plan-035-guided-round--2026-09-28)).

Maintainer decision (2026-09-28), after comparing Cap: keep the display and system awake during a session; when the Mac sleeps anyway, stop and save the recording instead of failing it; show notifications that fall during sleep after waking. Assumed here, open to the maintainer's change before implementation: keeping awake has no setting and applies to every session.

A user-initiated sleep cannot be prevented: macOS does not let an app veto Apple menu → Sleep, closing the lid without an external display, the power button, or a low-battery or thermal sleep. Out of scope: continuing the same file after waking (`MediaRecorder` cannot swap tracks, so it would take split files and a merge); a display forced to sleep or a screen locked without system sleep, whose outcome is only recorded (it still fails the recording, which the maintainer accepted on 2026-09-28); a setting for keeping awake; Windows, where the same Electron API exists but nothing is claimed.

## Cap comparison

A static source review of Cap at [`20c224073bece3fbebed8acb631bd2df97cd6f40`](https://github.com/CapSoftware/Cap/tree/20c224073bece3fbebed8acb631bd2df97cd6f40) (2026-09-27), not execution:

- **No keep-awake.** No `IOPMAssertion`, `caffeinate`, `SetThreadExecutionState` or keep-awake dependency exists in the repository, so a Cap recording can reach idle sleep and display sleep.
- **Sleep awareness without stopping.** [`power_observer.rs`](https://github.com/CapSoftware/Cap/blob/20c224073bece3fbebed8acb631bd2df97cd6f40/apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/power_observer.rs) observes will-sleep, did-wake and the screens' sleep and wake; asleep, it pauses the permission, camera and microphone watchers, and 0.5 s after waking it re-prewarms ScreenCaptureKit and refreshes devices. It neither stops nor saves a recording.
- **Restart after a system stop.** [`screen_capture/macos.rs`](https://github.com/CapSoftware/Cap/blob/20c224073bece3fbebed8acb631bd2df97cd6f40/crates/recording/src/sources/screen_capture/macos.rs) treats ScreenCaptureKit's system-stopped error by rebuilding the capture after 2 s, up to 3 times, into the same recording with a gap; when the display is gone or the restarts fail, the recording ends with the message quoted above. Crash-recovery recording is on by default, writing fragmented segments that survive an interruption.

RecordStuff adopts the sleep and wake awareness and a message that names the cause, and adds what Cap lacks: keeping awake during the session. It does not adopt the restart, for the `MediaRecorder` reason above; its partials are already playable (N31's 8.9 s partial decoded), so stopping cleanly is the smaller change.

## Implementation contract

- [x] **Keep awake.** A new `src/main/keep-awake.ts` holds one Electron `powerSaveBlocker` of type `prevent-display-sleep` from `starting` until the state settles in `idle` or `needsPermission`, so the countdown, the recording and the save are covered; display sleep would end the capture too, and the display assertion also keeps idle system sleep away. It never holds two, releases on every path back to a settled state (saved, failed, cancelled, a failed start) and on `will-quit`, and logs each start and stop with the blocker id. A blocker that fails to start is logged and never affects the recording. Main drives it from the Recorder's state events, which autorecord also passes through.
- [x] **Stop and save on sleep.** `Recorder.systemWillSleep()` runs in main's `powerMonitor` `suspend` handler right after the tray's notification hold, which is only a flag, and before any logging; `stop()` sends the stop to the host before the `stopping` state reaches subscribers. While recording it marks the session `stoppedEarly: "sleep"`, logs it and calls `stop()`: the capture host records a requested stop before the tracks end and then ignores the track end ([`sourceEnded`](../src/renderer/capture-host.ts)), so the file is finalized and published like any stop. During a countdown it cancels with reason `sleep` (no file, failure or notification); after `record` was sent it sets `stopOnStart`, as a late toggle does; while starting it cancels once prepared, as quit does. In stopping, idle and needsPermission it does nothing. If the tracks end first, today's failure path stays and the log says so. In the acceptance run macOS entered sleep about 5 s after `suspend` and the save took 13 ms. A save still running when the Mac sleeps completes after waking, but timers keep counting during sleep, so its stop deadline may already have passed.
- [x] **Types, text and records.** `EarlyStop` and `CancelReason` gain `sleep`. The saved notification reads "Saved {file}. Recording stopped because the Mac went to sleep." / 「已儲存 {file}。Mac 進入睡眠，已停止錄製。」 The session record's `stoppedEarly` accepts `sleep` in `src/shared/session-record.ts` and in [session-records.mts](../scripts/lib/session-records.mts), which now rejects anything but `lowDisk`; the recorder's and session log's lines read "(stopped early: the Mac went to sleep)", and [finalization-timing.mts](../scripts/lib/finalization-timing.mts) accepts both early-stop suffixes.
- [x] **Notifications after waking.** Between `suspend` and `resume`, `AppTray` holds every notification instead of showing it and logs `notification: held during sleep`; after `resume` it checks each second and shows the held ones in order once `powerMonitor.getSystemIdleTime()` is at most 2 s, and at once on `unlock-screen`. The first sleep test showed that timers keep counting during sleep (a 10 s fallback fired right at waking after under 7 s of awake time), and macOS wakes periodically for maintenance without the display, so no timer alone may release them and a maintenance wake, which has no input, shows nothing. The notification switch and support checks still apply when they are shown, and `destroy` drops held ones.
- [x] **Documentation.** In both languages: the recording design (the session's end on sleep and keeping awake), the desktop design (notifications held during sleep, the saved notification's sleep note and the new log lines), a design-decision row (keep awake during sessions, stop and save on sleep, no restart, with the Cap comparison), the function reference and the acceptance guide's recording rows.

## Verification and exclusions

- [x] Unit tests: keep-awake starting once across starting, countdown, recording and stopping, releasing on idle, needsPermission, a failed start, a cancel and dispose, never holding two, and logging a blocker that throws; `systemWillSleep` in each state (recording saves with `stoppedEarly: "sleep"` on the saved event and in the session record, a countdown is cancelled with reason `sleep` and no failure, arming sets `stopOnStart`, starting cancels once prepared, stopping, idle and needsPermission do nothing); the capture host keeping a requested stop normal when a track ends afterwards; the saved notification in both languages; the session log and records accepting `sleep`; the finalization parser reading both suffixes; `AppTray` holding during suspend, showing nothing after a resume without input or after an hour on timers alone, showing in order once input is recent or on unlock, applying the switch then, not holding when awake, and dropping held ones on destroy.
- [x] `pnpm check`.
- [x] On a fresh `pnpm start:app` bundle, agent-operated: during a countdown and a recording, `pmset -g assertions` lists a `PreventUserIdleDisplaySleep` assertion from RecordStuff; after a save, a cancel, a failure (read-only folder) and quit it is gone. The idle display-off run was not made: Chrome and another Electron app held display assertions, so it could not show RecordStuff's effect; the per-process assertion is the evidence ([record](../docs/verification/history-2026-09.md#plan-050-keep-awake-and-stop-on-sleep--2026-09-28)).
- [x] Maintainer-operated, because waking needs the maintainer's password or Touch ID: Apple menu → Sleep during a recording, then wake: a normal `.mp4` is saved and plays, no failure is added, the log shows the sleep stop before the tracks end, and the saved notification with the sleep note appears after waking. Sleep during a countdown: nothing is saved and nothing is shown. Record, not gate: a display forced to sleep (`pmset displaysleepnow`) and a locked screen during a recording.
- [x] Exclusions: Windows; low-battery and thermal sleep, which take the same `suspend` path; the quality and audio matrices, since capture and encoding are unchanged; the CPU budget, since the blocker adds no timer and the user-return check runs once a second only between a wake and the user's return.
