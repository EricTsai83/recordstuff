## Goal and product gate

Make future recordings editable after capture, while ordinary direct MP4 stays the default: after a recording, choose where the camera's bubble sits and how large it is, and set the system's and the microphone's volumes apart, then export an ordinary MP4. Old flattened recordings cannot recover camera or clean-screen pixels and are not promised to.

**D1 — whether and in what scope: decided 2026-10-24 by the maintainer**, from step 1's figures (the engine +3.33 points of a core, the app an estimated 1.27× the direct mode's CPU, +1.3 MiB, 1.33× the disk). Go on, in this scope only: the camera's place and size and the two volumes after recording. Mouse events and automatic zoom are dropped: the cursor stays drawn into the screen's picture as today. Styling of the screen's picture (backgrounds, padding, rounded corners, shadows, other output shapes), removed with 086, stays out; the maintainer does not want it. The camera bubble's own shape (size, rounding, mirror) is the existing camera layout's and stays.

## Steps and ownership

- [x] **Prototype the cost first.** An explicit editable-capture option, off by default, captures clean screen video, an optional separate camera and independent system and microphone tracks on a common media clock, with offsets, pause/stop rules and loss metadata, reusing the engine's encoders and muxer. Measure it before building UI; do not also encode a flattened MP4 unless the budget and D1 allow it, since the rendered export below makes one on demand. *Done 2026-10-24, behind the development switch `RECORDSTUFF_CAPTURE_EDITABLE=1` (macOS): one MP4 with the screen alone, each input's sound and the camera (1280×720) on tracks of their own, the first of each kind presented; `pnpm bench:editable` measured the engine +3.33 points of a core, the app estimated at 1.27× the direct mode's CPU, +1.3 MiB resident and 1.33× the disk a minute, within the proposed budgets. Loss is told as today (events, log, the session record), not yet written into the file; the app has no pause. What was verified and what was not is in the [record](../docs/verification/history-2026-10.md#plan-087-step-1-the-editable-recordings-cost--2026-10-24).*
- ~~**Mouse events, opt-in and bounded.**~~ Dropped by D1 (2026-10-24): no automatic zoom needs them, and the cursor stays in the screen's picture.
- [ ] **The editable option.** A Recording setting, off by default and explained where it is turned on, replaces step 1's development switch: with it on (macOS) recordings are saved editable, with it off they are as today. The recording's card says it is editable; such a file opened anywhere shows the screen with the system's sound until it is exported (step 1's track layout). Windows keeps the option hidden until its engine supports it.
- [ ] **A small pure scene model** (from 086) in Rust: from the edit's revision and a rational media time it returns the render parameters, here only the camera's place, size and visibility over the screen's picture. Time stays integer/rational to the platform boundary and is never advanced by UI frame deltas; coordinate spaces, rounding, aspect and crop are defined once; colour is BT.709 with its range carried through. It reuses `camera_layout`'s math rather than copying it, holds no capture clock or OS handle, and is checked by deterministic fixtures replayed forward, backward and in random order.
- ~~**Automatic zoom in that model.**~~ Dropped by D1 (2026-10-24).
- [ ] **The camera and the volumes in 085's edit view.** For an editable recording: the camera's bubble placed and sized on the preview as the Studio places it before a recording (one place for the whole recording), shown or hidden, and the system's and the microphone's volumes with the Studio's faders, each change undoable within 085's bounded edit state and saved in its versioned record. No timeline of keyframes or per-part placement.
- [ ] **Rendered preview and export** (from 086) in 085's export worker, on the existing WGSL compositor that already runs on Metal and Direct3D 11 (plan 075); wgpu is reconsidered only if multipass rendering the kernel cannot do becomes necessary, after measuring its cost. Preview: rendered on demand and while playing at the Studio's preview rate, sent over the existing encoded preview transport with 084's bounds, a generation per seek. Audio: the system and microphone tracks are decoded, placed on the common media clock by their offsets (a dropout is silence, never a shift), cut with 085's sample-exact rule and faded joins, mixed by the engine's existing mixer with the per-input volumes the edit sets, starting from the Studio's at recording (plans 074 and 075), and encoded to one AAC track; the preview plays the same mix, so what is heard while editing is what is exported, and no player is relied on to mix separate tracks. Export: hardware decode → scene render → hardware H.264 encode → mux, with finite frame and byte queues and backpressure; without hardware support the export is refused with a reason. Publication, cancellation, admission (recording always wins) and quit follow 085 unchanged; a job is bound to one edit revision.
- [ ] **Multi-track recovery.** 085's edit state and export worker extend to a session of several files: one session owner, per-track size and backlog limits, an aggregate disk-space policy, the manifest committed last and recovery metadata written incrementally (never rewriting a growing JSON array per event). After a worker's death the recoverable screen fragments are kept and missing camera, microphone and time gaps marked; disk exhaustion ends the media safely under the existing outcome contract.

## Gates and verification

Before implementation freeze ceilings against the pipeline baseline on the reference machine. Proposed for the prototype: total app CPU at most 1.5× the direct mode's with the same camera and audio inputs, at most 256 MiB more resident memory, and queues bounded independently of the take's length; disk amplification reported per track. These are proposed product budgets, not evidence. If exceeded, reduce the tracks or resolution or stop at D1; never loosen the default recording's budget.

The default mode stays within the baseline's spread and its cadence and sync budgets. The editable mode meets the existing sync and cadence criteria on every enabled track, with `pnpm matrix -- long` since several clocks can drift. Scene parameters match within stated tolerances in every replay order, and preview and export render the same frame at the same media time within tolerances declared before testing (no UI pixel baselines). The rendered export of a frozen 1080p30 reference runs at least in real time on the reference Mac with queue memory independent of length and cancel within 2 s at p95; decode, render, encoder wait and write are reported apart, and no speed gain may come from lower fidelity. On a fixture with distinct tones in the system and microphone tracks, both stay audible in the export and in sync with the picture across every cut, including a microphone dropout. Checks cover the camera's place at every seek, camera and microphone dropout and recovery after process death, then edit → export → native playback. Permissions follow the existing hands-on boundaries; TCC is never reset automatically.

Broad capture, storage, UI and lifecycle integration requires `pnpm check`, the final `pnpm acceptance:regression`, a fresh signed recording with media verification and playback, the affected capture-settings, Studio and lifecycle cases, the CPU comparison and the relevant matrix including `long`. Windows CI checks contracts and build only; untested hardware behaviour is listed. Close with the recovery, format and privacy documentation and bilingual help, failed experiments kept, probes removed and durable decisions updated.
