## Plan 075 closure — 2026-10-18

Plan 075 put the user's camera into recordings. Redesigned on 2026-10-17 at the maintainer's request after OBS's model, the engine composes the camera into the frames on the GPU before the encoder instead of recording a floating window, and a new Recording Studio tab arranges it on a live preview, before and during a recording. The design is in [recording](../system-design/recording.md#camera), [desktop](../system-design/desktop.md#recording-studio) and [camera permission](../system-design/desktop.md#camera-permission), the decisions in [design decisions](../system-design/decisions.md) and the CPU budget in [tooling](../system-design/tooling.md#cpu-budget).

- **Behaviour (step 1, D1–D21).** A bubble of free width, height (5–60% of the shorter side) and rounding, placed per display, mirrored only if chosen; off by default, asked for when turned on; refused at start as `camera_unavailable`; changeable while recording from the next frame; hidden and shown from the tray or the Studio; lost after 2 s without frames, the recording going on without it. Changed from the first recommendations at the maintainer's choice: the Live preview button (D5), the preview as a small H.264 stream (D17), the Recording Studio (D19), Live preview sharing the recording's session (D20), and per-input volume faders with the preview at 10 fps (D21).
- **Prototype gate (step 2).** Core Image composing cost about 10 points of a core at 30 fps; Metal compute about 4 at 30 fps and 7 at 60. The preview's first form (NV12 frames drawn with WebGL) cost 12.7 points; a 640-pixel H.264 stream decoded with WebCodecs passed with the preview's share accepted by the maintainer.
- **Engine (steps 5–6).** AVFoundation on macOS, choosing macOS's preferred camera and skipping a suspended one for System default; Media Foundation's source reader on Windows. One WGSL kernel translated by naga to Metal and HLSL; on Windows Direct3D 11 compute writing plane textures drawn into NV12 render targets, with a WARP path. Frames without a camera, or hidden, are the screen's byte for byte. The camera's age in its frames is logged at each stop.
- **Studio (step 7).** Preview across the top, Camera, Audio (meters, mutes, faders) and Recording controls under it; the sidebar collapses to a rail of icons. Found and fixed in the rounds: System default opening a different camera in the engine than in Settings (both now ask `systemPreferredCamera`, skipping a suspended camera), and a running app that kept showing camera access denied after the user allowed it (the card now offers Already allowed? Relaunch RecordStuff, the microphone's too).
- **Measurement (step 8).** See verification below.

## Plan 078 closure — 2026-10-17

076 and 074 left questions about the native capture engine: frames 50 ms apart, the development app recording its own windows, no per-frame diagnostic, decisions the Chromium path left behind, Windows UI shards that timed out closing the app, and a macOS microphone that was never taken back after reconnecting. Plan 078 closed them. The design is in [recording](../system-design/recording.md), [audio quality §19](../system-design/audio-quality.md#19-microphone-results-on-coreaudios-hal--2026-10-17), [tooling](../system-design/tooling.md) and [desktop](../system-design/desktop.md).

- **Step 1, frame gaps.** A per-frame log showed ScreenCaptureKit skipping about five refreshes per 15 s at 60 Hz, delivering neither a picture nor an idle frame; when the skipped refresh was a slot's, the file had a 50 ms gap and then a 17 ms one. A first guess, jitter at the slot boundary, was tried and reverted on the log's evidence. The engine now encodes the newest picture at a skipped slot (`Pacer::skipped`, `fill_skipped`, with a test that fails without it). `pnpm matrix -- quick` on the signed bundle: 0.44–0.66% of 30 fps frames 50 ms apart on the same build without the fill, back to back; 0.00% in all three cases with it, the longest gap 33 ms, 30.05–30.12 fps, flash-to-beep −6, −6 and −22 ms, recording CPU about 10%.
- **Step 2, own windows.** ScreenCaptureKit lists an application only while it owns a window, and a menu-bar app with nothing open owns none; every signed-bundle recording so far had a countdown overlay. Main now makes one hidden 1-pixel `BaseWindow` before capture is prepared. Both builds then logged `1 own app(s) excluded` in all three matrix takes, without a countdown; the not-found warning, if it ever appears, carries the evidence of why.
- **Step 3, diagnostics (the maintainer's decision, 2026-10-17: a development switch).** `RECORDSTUFF_FRAME_LOG=<folder>` writes `<file>.frames.jsonl` there for each recording, every delivery and encoded frame with the pacer's decision, and `pnpm diagnose:frames` summarizes it; no setting or UI.
- **Step 4, what the Chromium path left.** Windows encodes with the GPU's hardware H.264 transform when it can (adapter by LUID, never WARP or AMD, a cached grey self-test per adapter, driver and size, a watchdog, silent fallback before the first sample, `RECORDSTUFF_SOFTWARE_ENCODER=1` to force software). A Windows without Media Foundation is `media_components_missing`, checked at launch (Start recording unavailable, Open Optional features in the tray and Settings; exit code 4 when the module cannot load). `mp4_unsupported` was removed. Settings offers Reset failure history for an unreadable history, keeping the file as a backup. The frame-rate downgrade and unconfirmed resolution-cap notices were removed, and the size estimate no longer doubles at 60 fps.
- **Step 5, Windows closes.** Each phase of a fixture's close is timed into `test-results/ui-close-timings.jsonl` and summarized by CI's "Slowest closes" step. On Windows main exited 70–100 ms into a close while Playwright's own close waited up to 7.7 s for helper processes. A normal close is now main's exit, raced against Playwright's close, with a 15 s helper wait on Windows. Run 38953840602: no close killed on either platform, Windows' slowest 3.5 s (median 215–394 ms per shard).
- **Step 6, the microphone (the maintainer's decision, 2026-10-17: take back the same device only).** macOS captures through an AUHAL unit (CoreAudio's HAL) instead of `AVCaptureSession`, buffers from a preallocated pool on the real-time thread, AudioConverter to 48 kHz. The desktop round found that a device that leaves does not stop its unit: the HAL moves it to another input, which kept delivering sound (an iPhone microphone and a FreeClip 2 headset each followed by another microphone's room, 2.9 million frames dropped at the wrong rate), so the loss was never told. The reader now checks every 250 ms that the unit still records its device, closes it at once when it does not, and reopens the same UID when the HAL lists it, backing off from 2 to 30 s. Windows opens the same endpoint ID again after `AUDCLNT_E_DEVICE_INVALIDATED` (CI compilation only). The maintainer also decided on 2026-10-17 that a lost microphone is never replaced by another during a recording.
- **Cameras, for 075.** `probe_cameras_coming_and_going` (CoreMediaIO's and AVFoundation's lists once a second without a run loop) saw an iPhone's Continuity Camera in both lists but no change in 180 s while it was disconnected from the iPhone and chosen again, so whether the lists follow a camera without a run loop is not settled; 075's step 2 repeats it with a USB camera.
- **New plan.** The round raised microphone controls the maintainer wants later: an input level meter in Settings, a Search for devices button, a device list that follows connections, and whether a user may switch microphones during a recording ([079](../../plans/079-microphone-controls.md)).

## Plan 074 closure — 2026-10-16

A recording held system audio only; a narrated screen recording needed a second app. Plan 074 adds the microphone, off by default, mixed into the one AAC track through 076's engine. The design is in [recording](../system-design/recording.md), [audio quality](../system-design/audio-quality.md), [why recording left Chromium](../system-design/webrtc.md) and [desktop](../system-design/desktop.md).

- **Decisions (2026-10-16, the maintainer).** A Microphone switch in a new Audio section of the Recording tab, plus a Record microphone checkbox in the tray's idle menu; an Input device picker (System default or a listed input); with the switch on, a denied permission or an input that cannot open refuses the start before any countdown (`microphone_unavailable`); a microphone lost mid-recording leaves the recording going with system audio only; the raw microphone is mixed at unity gain with the unprocessed system audio, without the platform's voice processing, and headphones are the advice for speaker echo.
- **Departures from the plan's text.** `microphone_unavailable` is not grouped with the screen-permission failures, whose Open System Settings opens the screen pane; the macOS denial instead has its own action on the Microphone card and the denial notification. A chosen device that is not connected falls back to the system default with a prepared warning, as the picker alternative allows, instead of refusing. The tray item is absent outside idle rather than disabled. `pnpm autorecord` gained `microphone` (default off). The opened device's name is in the capture record (`CaptureReport.microphone`) but not in the capture log line, whose format `pnpm verify` reads.
- **Engine.** A microphone that delivers nothing for 2 s while recording is reported lost once; its later sound is dropped, not mixed back in, and a conversion (macOS) or pump (Windows) error on it loses the microphone instead of failing the session. Windows falls back to the default endpoint when the chosen one is missing or inactive and maps failures after `Initialize` to `microphone_unavailable`. The device list runs in a short-lived utility process of its own, only while no recording is being made, and is cancelled by a start.
- **Packaging.** `NSMicrophoneUsageDescription` and the `com.apple.security.device.audio-input` entitlement, both confirmed with `codesign -d --entitlements` and the bundle's Info.plist. The development Electron has no usage description, so macOS refuses it without a prompt: `pnpm audio:quality -- record` cannot record the microphone, and the fidelity runs below used the packaged app with `audio:quality -- verify`.
- **Learning article.** [Audio clocks, mixing and A/V sync](../learning/audio-clocks-mixing-and-av-sync.html), the one whose sources (076 and 074) have now all closed; "media permissions and privacy" waits for 075.

### After closure: comparison with OBS, Chromium and Cap (2026-10-16)

At the maintainer's request, what the desktop rounds could not cover (Windows, a silent microphone, device removal, the clocks) was compared, by reading code only, with obs-studio `1ad6110`, Chromium `e545d1e` and Cap `d7d36a3`; Cap's AGPL code was read for design only. Every finding was checked against our code before any change.

- **Defects fixed.** An audio-encoder error reached first by the microphone's sound was told as a lost microphone on both platforms; it now fails the recording (`capture_failed`), with a test. Windows trusted the microphone's QPC times: a driver giving positions seconds off would have dropped the whole microphone unnoticed or filled the track with silence far ahead; as Chromium does (crbug.com/1439283), a device whose first packet is more than 500 ms from its arrival, and any packet flagged `AUDCLNT_BUFFERFLAGS_TIMESTAMP_ERROR`, is timed by arrival (`PacketTimes`, with a test).
- **The maintainer's decisions, built.** A lost microphone is taken back when its sound returns (`microphoneResumed`; the notice now says the microphone dropped out and only system audio was recorded while it was out). Two or more inputs summing past −1 dBFS are softly limited instead of clipping, differently on each platform before; one input, or a quieter sum, is unchanged. A disagreement just past the 10 ms tolerance (by up to 2 ms) is corrected by one frame per buffer instead of a 10 ms jump, which at 100 ppm was a hole or cut about every 100 s. The microphone is captured in one channel and heard in both, so an audio interface's input 1 is not left-only. The stop log counts each input's corrections and the limited samples.
- **Hardening.** On Windows the microphone stream opts out of system ducking (`SetDuckingPreference`), and `E_ACCESSDENIED` is reported as the privacy setting.
- **Desktop round on the changed engine** (Claude ran it; the maintainer acted). The Settings switch raised macOS's prompt, allowed. `pnpm acceptance` with the microphone on passed: 10 flashes, 9 beeps (the first fell at the file's start, before any silence, so the detector cannot count it), integrity and the cancel case. On an iPhone microphone take, left minus right was −inf dB over 35 s: the microphone is mono in both channels. Disconnecting the iPhone microphone once and a HUAWEI FreeClip 2 Bluetooth headset twice: each told lost after 2 s, the recording went on, the saved record carried `microphoneLost`, the new notice appeared at the end, and video and audio ran equally long (362.84 s, 135.45 s, 154.72 s).
- **Not taken back after reconnecting.** Connected again, even as the default input, neither device was taken back, through AVFoundation's connection notifications (none ever arrived) or a once-a-second `uniqueID` lookup in a build tried during the round; that code was removed. A probe outside the app, polling once a second, showed why: without a run loop CoreAudio's device list dropped the headset at once while `AVCaptureDevice(uniqueID:)` and a fresh `DiscoverySession` still reported it connected 27 s later; with a run loop on the main thread AVFoundation followed at once. AVFoundation keeps its device state on the main run loop, which the engine's utility process does not run. Cap avoids it by capturing through cpal (CoreAudio) in a Tauri process that has the run loop; Chromium's audio service, outside the browser process like our engine, captures with CoreAudio AUHAL and listens with `AudioObjectAddPropertyListener`. Reopening through CoreAudio became 078's step 6 ([closure](#plan-078-closure--2026-10-17)); until then only a stall of the same session is taken back (unit-tested, not seen on hardware).
- **Checks.** `pnpm check` (152 files, 1848 tests; `cargo test --lib` 74, with new cases for the encoder error, packet times, one-frame correction, the limiter and resuming), Windows-target `cargo check --tests` and clippy, documentation links and the learning article's validator. The bundle of the round still held the removed reconnection code; the final source differs from it only by that removal, built and tested again. Review: Codex GPT-6.1 Sol, read-only, two passes. Pass 1: the microphone's loss and return could be told out of order, and the watchdog could take a stale delivery time for silence and mark the recording, so the delivery time, the lost state and both events now share one lock; the limiter could reach exactly full scale in `f32` for loud sums, so it is held just below it, with a strict test. Pass 2 confirmed both fixes and found nothing new.

Not done: listening by ear; the Windows microphone (CI only); reopening a reconnected microphone (078); fidelity, sync and CPU again after the limiter, drift and mono changes (078 step 6); the cause of the 3.4-point CPU difference.

## Plan 076 closure — 2026-10-16

Recording ran through Chromium's `getDisplayMedia` and `MediaRecorder` in a hidden renderer, which records one display or one window and can leave out only this app's protected windows; it cannot hide the menu bar or Dock, record an area or chosen apps, or one app's sound. Plan 076 replaced that path on macOS and Windows with a native Rust engine (napi-rs) in an Electron `utilityProcess` that captures, encodes, muxes and writes the file, and removed the Chromium path. The design is in [recording](../system-design/recording.md), [architecture](../system-design/architecture.md) and the ten native rows of [design decisions](../system-design/decisions.md); [why recording left Chromium](../system-design/webrtc.md) is the design record of the old path.

- **Decisions (2026-10-14, the maintainer).** D1 Rust on both platforms; D2 one `utilityProcess` per attempt; D3 the engine writes the file, main keeping its name, folder, free-space check, sentinel, history and notifications; D4 the platform's encoders (VideoToolbox and AudioToolbox; Media Foundation) with a hybrid MP4 muxer written for the project, FFmpeg kept out of recording; D5 Cap's MIT crates may be adapted, its AGPL code only read; D6 a "Hide menu bar and Dock" switch, macOS only, off by default; D7 the Chromium path behind a development flag until macOS acceptance, then removed; D8 Windows verified by CI only. On 2026-10-15 the maintainer declined starting the capture process ahead of time (about 50 ms for an idle process of about 73 MB).
- **Prototype gate (2026-10-14, M1 Pro, Chromium in brackets).** Shortcut to `prepared` 457–535 ms (347–369 ms); audio and picture equally long in every file, 10 minutes included (+13 to +15 ms); 10-minute drift 0 ms over 600 flash–beep pairs; killing the capture process mid-recording left an 8.0 s playable partial file. ## Plan 072 closure — 2026-10-14

On 2026-10-13 the maintainer saw a renderer of the installed app at 74–77% of a core while its player played a 2 h 21 min recording, and 0% once paused. Plan 072 named two causes, fixed both and added playback to the CPU budget ([tooling](../system-design/tooling.md#cpu-budget), playback costs).

- **Container.** The long recording had stayed a fragmented MP4: the capture host can flush an empty audio run with `tfdt` 0 at stop, and `FragmentIndex` failed the whole file on it (`track 1 goes back to 0, before its last sample`). A `traf` whose runs hold no samples is now skipped, so such recordings save as plain MP4; two unit cases cover it, including the ffmpeg packet comparison with an empty final fragment. Playing a fragmented file costs the renderer more than its length: Chromium's demuxer reads every `moof` through a `recordstuff-media:` range request and the media buffer keeps thousands of disjoint ranges. In the same Electron the same file costs 3.5% from `file://` and 72.8% through a handler serving ranges as `RecordingsLibrary.handle` does; cache validators changed nothing, a 206 capped at 4 MiB did not play, and `net.fetch(file://)` (4.4%) ignores `Range`. The maintainer chose no repair for files already saved fragmented (2026-10-14): the 47 local ones were test recordings and went to the Trash. Files saved fragmented by earlier versions keep the cost and their slow opens.
- **Blur.** On a plain file the Settings player cost more than full screen and Chrome, in the GPU process, because the dialog backdrop's `backdrop-filter` is redrawn with every video frame. With the maintainer's agreement (2026-10-14), after comparing a darker backdrop without blur, the backdrop now only darkens and ui.css blurs the page under it (`#root`, `filter: blur(3px)` while `.player[data-open]`), which Chromium draws once; the look is the same.
- **Decoder.** Every file, fragmented or plain, decodes video with `VideoToolboxVideoDecoder` and audio with `FFmpegAudioDecoder`, so software decoding was not a cause.
- **Prevention.** `pnpm measure:cpu` gained scenario P: the last 30 fps recording, opened from the tray's Show last recording, its card's Play button confirmed by element id through Chromium's accessibility tree (turned off again before the window), played with Return and judged over 50 s per role: app ≤22%, GPU process ≤11%, renderers ≤10%, a floor of 0.5% each as evidence that something played, and the file saved as plain MP4. `--skip-playback` leaves it out.
