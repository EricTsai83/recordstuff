## Plan 096 closure — 2026-10-29

Plan 096 (optional removal of speaker echo from the microphone of editable recordings) closed at its stop 2 on 2026-10-29, the maintainer's decision after listening to real takes and reviewing what other recorders do. Claude ran steps 0–3 with read-only Codex GPT-6.1 Sol reviews; the measurement tools, the candidates and the plan as it stood are in Git at `ef44939b` and `d49232f9`, and were then removed, so nothing of it ships or stays in the tree. The decision: [design decisions](../system-design/decisions.md).

- **What was measured.** Each candidate processed the microphone with the recorded system track as its reference, and was judged by gates frozen before any candidate ran (D1, 2026-10-29): echo removed by at least 25 dB (overall, in each band from 125 Hz to 12 kHz, and within 2 s); without echo the microphone unchanged (difference at most −40 dB); with the voice and the speakers at once, the voice's loss at most 1 dB median and 3 dB at the 95th percentile, with at most 1% of its frames cut by more than 10 dB; at least 20 times real time. Stage A was 15 simulated rooms with known answers (macOS `say` voices and synthetic music, through the app's AAC at 256 kbps, D3); stage B was the maintainer's own speakers and microphones, never used for tuning: 21 takes on the MacBook Pro's built-in speakers and microphone and 21 on external speakers on the headphone jack with the BenQ GW2785TC's microphone (speech and music at 50% and 100%, a volume step, the output muted for 3 s), each composed with the maintainer's read voice.
- **Real speakers.** The echo is loud enough to matter: on the built-in pair it sat at −6.8 to +5.8 dB against the voice (median per take). The system track is captured before the output volume and the mute (it stayed at −20 dB while the output was muted and the microphone fell 13–15 dB), so the recorded system track is a usable reference whatever the volume (D2).
- **WebRTC AEC3** (`webrtc-audio-processing` 2.1, the default settings and gentler near-end settings): 0 of 57 conditions. Echo was removed well (median 50–68 dB), but it changed the microphone where there was no echo (−16 dB, its band split; −25 to −29 dB with the fixed phase removed, reported only) and its suppressor cut the voice whenever both sounded (frames cut by more than 10 dB: median 45% simulated, 26% built-in, 5% external).
- **Our offline canceller** (pure Rust: delay by cross-correlation, one partitioned frequency-domain filter per stereo channel, three passes, the microphone left bit for bit where the system track is silent, suppression only to the room's noise floor): 11 of 15 simulated, **1 of 21 built-in, 0 of 21 external**. On real speakers what the filters could not model (speaker distortion, the MacBook's body) left a residual as loud as the voice, and the second stage could not tell them apart: where it removed the echo it cut the voice (built-in music at 100%: up to 65.5% of voice frames cut by more than 10 dB; external speech at 100%: about 27%), and where it kept the voice it left echo (external music: 11–25 dB removed). Cost was within bounds: about 34 times real time, about 455 KB of code, about 0.4 MB of state.
- **Other recorders** (documents and source, not run here): Cap's desktop app, OBS, Camtasia, Loom and Screen Studio do not remove speaker echo; they advise headphones and keep the microphone and system sound apart. Meeting tools and recording SDKs (Granola, Recall.ai, RecordKit's WebRTC AEC3 option) do, for intelligibility rather than fidelity.
- **Review.** Codex passes over each step: the analyzer's convergence windows, a zero noise floor and the CLI's exit code (step 1); timers, the guided page's launcher, undetectable echo and the capture stage's reference (step 2); a phase correction that would have weakened a frozen gate, now reported only, and a stale build (AEC3; its second pass found nothing); the canceller's noise floor, stereo and polarity handling, the delay search's window and the plan's understated worst cases (first pass), digital silence and the plan's stage A failures (second). All accepted and fixed before the numbers above.
- **Not tried.** Open neural echo models (DTLN-aec, MIT; LocalVQE, Apache-2.0; both 16 kHz, a few MB plus a runtime) as the second stage only, and macOS voice processing (live only, ducks other apps' sound, the very sound being recorded). Either would need new real-speaker takes to judge. The listening clips and stage B material were local and deleted with the maintainer's test recordings.

## Plan 098 closure — 2026-10-28

Plan 098 (every sound heard when an editable recording is played) closed on 2026-10-28. Claude implemented it on `8b9bfa21` (uncommitted, as requested) with two read-only Codex GPT-6.1 Sol review passes, and ran one desktop round after the maintainer's “好了” (reference M1 Pro, macOS 26.6.2, the BenQ GW2785TC as the primary display and the portrait BL2480T beside it, the maintainer's iPhone camera, the microphone on). Design: [editable recordings](../system-design/editing.md#editable-recordings), [decisions](../system-design/decisions.md), [`bench:editable`](../system-design/tooling.md#export-benchmark).

- **What was built.** With both inputs an editable recording has three sound tracks: the inputs mixed exactly as a plain recording mixes them (the first, enabled one), then the system's and the microphone's own as disabled alternates; each input is placed on the shared clock by each mixer it feeds, and the late frames and corrections it reports are the mix's. Its `rsed` description is version 2 (`"mix": true`) only then; with one input it stays version 1 and one track. The TypeScript parser accepts versions 1 and 2 (a mix of exactly two different inputs); the engine's source index keeps every disabled sound track's place (at most two), so an interrupted recording with an empty system track keeps the microphone where it was. The edit view copies both inputs' own tracks (`firstSound`, `secondSound`) and plays the mix at gain 0 once both are there, as the full-screen viewer does; the export names its tracks (`soundTracks: [1, 2]`), never the mix. `bench:editable -- --pair mix` measures the mix's own cost; `acceptance:editor` checks three tracks, version 2, both preview copies and the export's named tracks.
- **Decisions and departures.** D1, the gates as proposed (M1 ≤ +1.0 point, M2 ≤ 1.5×, M3 ≤ +2.5 MB a minute, M4 ≤ +16 MiB, M5 reported); D2, version 1 recordings left as they are. The development switch that leaves the mix out is a method of the engine's test build (`Core::leave_out_mix`), not an environment variable, so no shipped engine can be told to drop it and nothing needs scrubbing. 3.0.0's handling of a version 2 file was read from the `v3.0.0` source, not run: it plays the enabled mix, lists the file as ordinary, and its engine refuses two disabled sound tracks, so its Edit… says the recording cannot be edited. The edit view's log names each preview copy with the file's track it came from.
- **M1, the gate** (`pnpm bench:editable -- --pair mix --repeat 5`, synthetic 1080p30 with a 720p camera and two tones, no desktop). The first run (3 rounds) read +4.11 points (5.97% → 10.08%) under a load average of 25–29 from other work; a direct timing then put the mix's AAC encoding at 400 ms of CPU in 60 s (0.67 points) and its summing at 26 ms. Re-run on a quiet machine at the maintainer's choice: **+0.84 points (11.53% → 12.37%), PASS**; rounds still moved by about two points. M3 +1.92 MB a minute (PASS), M4 +1.2 MiB (PASS), M5 3.96% → 3.44% (reported, noise). Evidence: `2026-10-27T19-22-16-353Z-editable-mix-098` (the failed run) and `2026-10-28T01-57-48-709Z-editable-mix-098-r2` (local only).
- **Correctness.** In the engine's mac tests the mix of the synthetic 440 and 660 Hz inputs holds each within 0.5 dB of its own track (measured −0.008 dB) at the same length; the export of a file with the mix equals the export of the same inputs without it sample for sample (worst difference 0).
- **Desktop round** (2026-10-28 15:05–15:27 local, a fresh signed `pnpm start:app` bundle). **M2** (`pnpm measure:cpu -- --camera preview=0 --skip-settings --skip-playback --minutes 1 --repeat 3`, back to back): editable 19.5%, 20.9%, 20.1% (median 20.1%), direct 13.6%, 13.5%, 13.9% (median 13.6%): **1.48×, within 087's 1.5×**; 087 measured 19.2% editable. App memory peaked at 378 MB editable and 362 MB direct; the encoder service 3.7% and 2.6%; the editable files held two pictures and three sound tracks, about 126 MB a minute against 63 MB. **The microphone in the enabled track**: on the 60 s editable file the decoded first sound track equalled the system's track plus the microphone's (least-squares weights 1.000 and 1.000, residual −104.8 dB against the microphone's −44.9 dB; per second, the mix less the system correlated with the microphone at 0.963–1.000). **QuickTime** (`pnpm acceptance:playback`): duration, size, real-time play, seeks, picture and play to the end passed; QuickTime plays the enabled track, the one measured; nobody listened. **`pnpm verify --sync`** on that file: A/V offset −4 ms over 58 flash/beep pairs, head and tail alike, but one flash more than a second from either end had no beep, so the check failed by its rule; the direct file of M2 matched only one flash (that material was not made for the sync check), incomplete. **`pnpm acceptance:editor`**: the first try, with the material full screen on the primary display, found no status item (the menu bar hides in full screen there), so it ran with `--secondary` as 087's did. Its second try passed every check through the export, including three sound tracks with version 2, both preview copies and sound tracks 1/2, then stopped at the timeline: the volume step left its stretch selected and, since 2026-10-26, a selection's actions cover the times in a narrow view. The runner now presses Deselect first; its third try passed 24 of 26 checks, the two left (the playhead read while a range is held and while skimming) failing for the same covering, a runner gap since that redesign, not this plan. Cleanup: RecordStuff and QuickTime exited, `settings.json` byte for byte as before (Editable recordings was on; M2's direct run turned it off and back), `caffeinate` stopped.
- **Checks.** `pnpm test:scope -- editing player` while working; `pnpm check` after the desktop round, with the runner's fix (214 files, 2345 TypeScript tests; 197 + 48 Rust tests, 11 ignored; the build and the engine's load); `cargo test --lib` 245 passed; Clippy and fmt clean. Mac VideoToolbox tests (`pipeline_test`, `edit_test`) failed under the heavy load and passed alone, as before this plan.
- **Review.** Two read-only Codex GPT-6.1 Sol passes at medium: the first, over the implementation, found nothing; the second, over the runner's Deselect fix and the documentation in both languages, found nothing either.
- **Not verified.** Playback in the Recordings tab and the full-screen viewer on the desktop (Chromium plays the enabled track, the one measured, but no round played it there); listening in any player; 3.0.0 running a version 2 file; an interrupted version 2 recording on the desktop (engine-tested); players other than QuickTime and Chromium; Windows, which has no editable recordings.

## Plan 087 closure — 2026-10-25

Plan 087 (optional editable capture: the camera's place and the volumes after recording) closed on 2026-10-25, the last of the [083–087 series](../../plans/README.md#glide-inspired-evolution-083087). The design is [editable recordings](../system-design/editing.md#editable-recordings); step 1 and D1 are [below](#plan-087-step-1-the-editable-recordings-cost--2026-10-24).

- **What was built.** A Recording setting, **Editable recordings** (macOS, off by default, locked while recording, hidden on Windows), replaces step 1's development switch; the card of such a recording says **Editable**. Its file carries a description (`moov/udta/rsed`). Its edit view adds the camera's bubble over the player, dragged or moved with the arrow keys, resized keeping its shape and shown or hidden, and the system's and microphone's volumes, each change saved in version 2 of the edit record and undone with the cuts; the preview plays the camera and second sound copied into userData beside the player and mixes the sounds through the engine's limiter curve in Web Audio. Its export is always rendered: VideoToolbox decodes, the recording's Metal compositor composes the bubble, every kept picture is encoded again, and the sounds are mixed at the edit's volumes, cut with 5 ms fades and encoded to one track, through 085's worker and publication.
- **Departures from the plan, decided while implementing.** D1 left one place for the whole recording, so the scene has no time: it is a checked record in [shared code](../../src/shared/editable.ts) and the engine's `RenderOptions`, not a time-indexed Rust model, and replaying it in different orders has nothing to test. The preview is not rendered by the engine over the Studio's encoded transport: the page plays the copied tracks in step with the player and draws the bubble over it with the same layout, which costs no encoding while editing; preview and export were compared by eye and by the export's measured bubble place, not within declared pixel tolerances. Recovery needed no multi-file session: the recording is one fragmented file, an interrupted one renders what it holds (a test), and an empty camera or second sound track counts as absent.

### After closure: comparison with OBS, Chromium and Cap (2026-10-16)

At the maintainer's request, what the desktop rounds could not cover (Windows, a silent microphone, device removal, the clocks) was compared, by reading code only, with obs-studio `1ad6110`, Chromium `e545d1e` and Cap `d7d36a3`; Cap's AGPL code was read for design only. Every finding was checked against our code before any change.

- **Defects fixed.** An audio-encoder error reached first by the microphone's sound was told as a lost microphone on both platforms; it now fails the recording (`capture_failed`), with a test. Windows trusted the microphone's QPC times: a driver giving positions seconds off would have dropped the whole microphone unnoticed or filled the track with silence far ahead; as Chromium does (crbug.com/1439283), a device whose first packet is more than 500 ms from its arrival, and any packet flagged `AUDCLNT_BUFFERFLAGS_TIMESTAMP_ERROR`, is timed by arrival (`PacketTimes`, with a test).
- **The maintainer's decisions, built.** A lost microphone is taken back when its sound returns (`microphoneResumed`; the notice now says the microphone dropped out and only system audio was recorded while it was out). Two or more inputs summing past −1 dBFS are softly limited instead of clipping, differently on each platform before; one input, or a quieter sum, is unchanged. A disagreement just past the 10 ms tolerance (by up to 2 ms) is corrected by one frame per buffer instead of a 10 ms jump, which at 100 ppm was a hole or cut about every 100 s. The microphone is captured in one channel and heard in both, so an audio interface's input 1 is not left-only. The stop log counts each input's corrections and the limited samples.
- **Hardening.** On Windows the microphone stream opts out of system ducking (`SetDuckingPreference`), and `E_ACCESSDENIED` is reported as the privacy setting.

## Plan 072 closure — 2026-10-14

On 2026-10-13 the maintainer saw a renderer of the installed app at 74–77% of a core while its player played a 2 h 21 min recording, and 0% once paused. Plan 072 named two causes, fixed both and added playback to the CPU budget ([tooling](../system-design/tooling.md#cpu-budget), playback costs).
