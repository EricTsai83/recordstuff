## Tray and notifications

Sources: [tray-model.ts](../../src/main/tray-model.ts), [tray.ts](../../src/main/tray.ts), [index.ts](../../src/main/index.ts).

TrayModel is a pure projection containing icon, title, tooltip, and a flat list of commands; preferences are not in the tray at all, so what the model returns is exactly what the menu shows. AppTray maps it to Electron without a separate business state machine. Left-click toggles recording; right-click builds the menu from current state and settings. Hovering over the tray icon shows the current status and a localized “Right-click to open the menu” hint in the native tooltip. It does not use setContextMenu, which would change left-click behavior on macOS. The [global shortcut](#recording-shortcut) calls the same toggle as the left click.

| State | Icon/title | Tray commands | Preferences in Settings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| needsPermission | Idle/empty | Permission guidance, settings/relaunch, reveal last recording when one was saved while permission was lost, output folder | All editable |
| idle | Idle/empty | Ready or folder unavailable; reveal last recording when its path exists in state; output folder | All editable |
| starting | Idle/`…` | Permission-prompt guidance | Language only |
| recording | Recording/`REC` | Stop (tooltip names the shortcut when registered); output folder greyed | Language only |
| stopping | Idle/`…` | Saving | Language only |

Every state offers Settings, Show log, and Quit, and Settings is never disabled: the panel itself says which preferences are locked. macOS uses template PNG/@2x assets and a title; Windows branches use ICO assets. REC may appear in full-display recordings; this is an accepted visibility tradeoff.

Native notifications are localized and silent. Saved notifications reveal the completed file; when the disk guard ended the recording they say so ("Saved {file}. Recording stopped early because the disk is almost full."), and the recording stays a success outside failure history ([recording design](recording.md#file-completion-and-failure)). Recording failure notifications always open Recording failures in Settings, where the confirmed outcome and recovery actions are shown. Quality/language save failures and frame-rate downgrade notifications are informational.

On macOS, a notification click does two things: it delivers the response to the app and asks the system to activate the notifying app, and the activation lands about 110 ms after the click callback. The reveal runs in setImmediate and asks Finder to select the file at once; when the system then makes the windowless app active, Finder is pushed back behind the user's previous window and nothing visible happens (the v0.1.0 report; on macOS 26.6 it hit about one click in three, rarely the first click of a process). Plan 014 therefore arms a one-shot `did-become-active` listener for `ACTIVATION_WINDOW_MS` (1 s) after the reveal: when the activation arrives inside that window, the file is revealed again from the now-active app so Finder's activation lands last. Logs distinguish `reveal requested`, `reveal repeated after activation` and `reveal failed`. Only the click arms the listener; a save in the background never touches Finder. Unsupported notifications and failed events are logged. OS notification preferences still decide whether a banner is visible. Native evidence is recorded by `pnpm acceptance:notification` ([tooling](tooling.md#notification-acceptance)). The notification icon was confirmed normal after the user's reboot on 2026-09-14.

`AppTray` retains each native `Notification` until `click`, `close`, or `failed`; `show` alone does not release it. Shutdown closes outstanding notifications and clears ownership; a synchronous `show()` failure releases the object and is logged. This keeps callbacks reachable after `show()` returns: Electron 44.3.0's [notification wrapper](https://github.com/electron/electron/blob/v44.3.0/shell/browser/api/electron_api_notification.cc) is garbage-collected and clears its native delegate on destruction. This lifecycle fix does not establish the cause of the historical undelivered click. There is no timer-based eviction; notifications without a terminal event remain owned until shutdown. Windows Action Center lifetime is not fixed or verified here: a Windows `close` event may be only a banner timeout while its history entry remains clickable.

Saved notifications are scheduled by `SavedNotification` after file finalization and idle, with one 500 ms timer on macOS; other platforms request immediately. This gives macOS time to clear the capture-related notification suppression state. It is a measured heuristic, not a readiness signal or delivery guarantee: Focus, another capture, and notification preferences still apply. Leaving idle (a new recording or a permission-recovery state) cancels the old pending notification permanently; permission guidance takes priority over that old save; quit admission cancels it and suppresses saves during that attempt. A deferred quit restores future delivery without replaying suppressed saves. No queue or retry is retained. Notification request exceptions are logged without affecting the saved file. Saving and idle never wait for notification delivery. See [timing evidence](../verification/history-2026-09.md#saved-notification-timing--2026-09-20).

## Settings window

Sources: [ui-model.ts](../../src/main/ui-model.ts), [settings-model.ts](../../src/main/settings-model.ts), [settings-window.ts](../../src/main/settings-window.ts), [renderer/settings.ts](../../src/renderer/settings.ts).

The tray keeps the commands that must stay one click away; every preference lives in one separate, sandboxed window. Settings opens it or focuses the existing one. On macOS the app focuses itself first, because a Dock-less app does not come forward when it merely shows a window. Changes save immediately and the window stays open; a language change relabels it and retitles it in place. Closing it — including with Escape or the platform close shortcut, which the panel handles itself because a Dock-less app has no application menu — leaves the menu-bar recorder running. The close shortcut is exactly ⌘W on macOS and Ctrl+W elsewhere, with no other modifier; one predicate serves the page and the shortcut editor, so macOS ⌃W and ⌘⇧W stay capturable. ⌘W itself never becomes the recording shortcut: the editor closes on it, and the validator reserves `CommandOrControl+W`, so a hand-edited settings file falls back to the default too. The typed character decides, as native menus do, and the physical W key counts only where the layout types no Latin letter there. If the page's renderer process dies, main disposes that window rather than keeping a blank one, and the next Settings open creates and loads a new window; it never reloads by itself, so a page that keeps crashing cannot loop. Close, blur and crash events act only on their own window instance, so a late event from a disposed window cannot clear its replacement. Capture still uses its own hidden renderer.

[settings-model.ts](../../src/main/settings-model.ts) declares every preference once, with stable group and choice ids, and is the only place that knows what a choice does. It produces both the view the panel renders and the answer to "is this request allowed right now". The tray model is a sibling projection of the same state and context, not a source the panel reads from.

Main owns committed preferences; the panel only retains pending user choices. It renders the view it is given and sends back a group id and a choice id — never an action. Main refuses any sender but the panel's own main frame, resolves the pair against a freshly built model, and only then calls the same action handler the tray uses, which re-checks the recording state before saving. Starting, recording and saving lock every preference except language and appearance, in the panel and again at that boundary. Saves are serialized in request order, so a second change waits instead of being reported as a failure. While saves are pending, controls display committed values from main, with non-layout save status and per-row aria-busy; newer user intent remains queued independently of older replies and pushes, and other groups remain locked until all requests settle. It then shows committed values, with an inline message when the latest choice did not take effect, and a shortcut the OS refused keeps its selection plus a note saying it is inert. A group may also carry `actions`: buttons rendered under its control, for a system pane that can override the preference in the same card. A choice in an actions group, or in that `actions` list, has no committed value to compare against, so its handler reports its own outcome as a boolean and that is used verbatim; every other choice is still judged by whether the requested value is the committed one, so a handler that reports nothing can never turn an unsaved change into a success. The control being changed stays live while its neighbours go inert, because a disabled element cannot hold keyboard focus. The preload exposes only read, choose, capture and change subscription; the one string the panel can need before its first view — a failed initial read — is localized from the language main puts in the page URL.

The Recording settings tab contains screen selection, video quality, resolution cap and frame rate, with explanations distinguishing encoding quality from pixel dimensions. General contains the shortcut, language and update controls. Tabs support arrow keys, Home and End and remain selected across preference changes. Update commands use buttons and report completion separately from saved preferences.

The 560×680 settings window (minimum 380×360) uses native window chrome and a fixed segmented tab strip above a scrolling, centred list. Main declares optional `control`, `section` and `noteKind` fields: Notifications and startup update checks are native checkbox switches; quality and language use native radio segments; other preferences retain native popup menus. Omitted fields preserve menu/explanation defaults. Consecutive section ids share an inset list; update rows share one heading and the locked Recording section has a footnote. Shared light/dark accent and focus tokens apply throughout, with native control fallbacks in forced colors.

Current blockers and historical recording failures are separate structured diagnostics, apart from ordinary help. A missing saved display remains a disabled, selected option with an Unavailable suffix. Only a current blocker with an available Primary alternative offers Use Primary display; it sends the original screen/primary ids through the existing save path and never records automatically. Failed saves stay beside the affected control; Retry save exists only while the candidate remains offered, unlocked and the committed value has not changed. Successful recovery confirms the saved choice without claiming capture has been tested. Recovery-button removal restores focus only when that button still owned focus; background pushes retain control identity and scroll position.

Shortcut editing uses one native menu containing one recommendation, the current custom value (if any), Off and Custom shortcut…. There is no separate edit button. A saved custom combination appears with a custom suffix; only the current value is retained, not a history. Selecting the recommendation replaces it without keeping a history; Off preserves it. Repeated arming is ignored while listening or awaiting acknowledgement. A key combination only previews a local draft; Confirm or unmodified Enter saves it. With a draft, Tab reaches Confirm and Cancel; without a draft, Tab exits capture. Cancel keeps the committed value. Explicit cancellation and a completed edit return focus to the menu; Tab and blur retain the user’s destination. Main acknowledges arming before listening; temporary suspension is not an OS conflict. Confirm preserves capture focus during left-button mouse-down so macOS cannot cancel the draft before click; keyboard activation and cancellation outside the editor are unchanged.

The native window title remains “RecordStuff - Settings” (“RecordStuff - 設置” in Traditional Chinese). Content starts with tabs, without a duplicate icon, large heading or autosave hint; a visually hidden h1 remains for accessibility. General ends with “Built by Eric Tsai” on the left and official-website (globe) / GitHub-source icon buttons on the right. Each 32px button has a localized accessible name and hover tooltip; diagnostic retry buttons retain their normal text dimensions. The renderer sends only `about/website` or `about/source`; main authorizes these fixed destinations, opens the default browser and reports opening failures. They remain available during recording and cannot navigate the settings document or supply arbitrary URLs.

Pointer interaction hides the focus outline without discarding DOM focus; Tab/arrow navigation restores the 2px focus line, using system colors in forced-colors mode. Menu options and action buttons reconcile by id without rebuilding the panel when custom values or update results change. Temporary save locks do not dim neighbouring radio segments. A three-bar listening indicator appears after main acknowledges capture; reduced motion makes it static. Actions such as opening notification settings show no Applying text, and only report an Action failed error on failure.

The scroll viewport normally hides its scrollbar and shows a non-interactive gradient glass cue without an arrow only when content remains below. It disappears at the bottom or when content fits, and recomputes after layout/content changes. Increased contrast or reduced transparency uses a solid boundary; forced colors restores a native scrollbar. Repeated update checks retain the previous result text and nodes while the check button changes label/availability, then replace the result in place instead of collapsing and expanding the card.

Window width/height are stored independently in userData/settings-window.json, outside recording preferences and their write queue. The initial 560×680 Electron logical pixels retain bilingual control width while reducing unused space after the header simplification. Resize events debounce writes by 250ms; close/shutdown synchronously flush the final dimensions for reopen and next-launch restoration. Minimum size is 380×360. Opening fits and centers the window in the work area of the display under the pointer, without persisting position or overwriting a saved size merely because that display is smaller. Missing/invalid geometry falls back to defaults. Failed writes are logged, retaining the in-process size without replacing the old file with partial JSON.

## Recording shortcut

Sources: [hotkey.ts](../../src/main/hotkey.ts), [shortcuts.ts](../../src/main/shortcuts.ts), [shared/hotkey.ts](../../src/shared/hotkey.ts), [index.ts](../../src/main/index.ts). Plan 016 added a global start/stop shortcut so recording can be toggled while another app is frontmost and so unattended acceptance has a system-level entry point on a process with no window.

RecordingHotkey wraps Electron `globalShortcut`. A press calls the same `toggle` function as a tray left click, so `Recorder.toggle()` remains the only decision point: it starts when idle, stops when recording, re-issues the permission notification in needsPermission, and ignores presses while starting or stopping. Every press is logged as `hotkey: <accelerator> pressed` before the toggle. `apply(settings)` releases the previous registration before registering the new one, so a change never leaves two combinations active; `dispose()` runs on will-quit.

The user chooses one recommended shortcut, Custom shortcut… or Off from **Settings → Shortcut** (idle/needsPermission only, like quality). The default is `CommandOrControl+Shift+1` (⌘⇧1 on macOS, Ctrl+Shift+1 elsewhere), set by maintainer decision on 2026-09-21. A global shortcut wins over the frontmost app, so the default has to be a combination no common app expects. The obvious mnemonic ⌘⇧R was rejected twice for that reason: the 2026-09-19 conflict check found it bound to hard reload in Chrome and Firefox, Reader in Safari and local recording in Zoom, so pressing it in a browser starts a screen recording instead of reloading. Existing users keep it as their current custom value; it is no longer a suggested option. Digits are the quieter range — macOS owns ⌘⇧3/4/5 for screenshots and screen recording, and apps bind plain ⌘1…9 for tabs and view modes rather than the shifted form — but ⌘⇧1 has not been through the same app-by-app check; that belongs to native acceptance. `CommandOrControl+Alt+Shift+R`, the previous default, was verified unbound in Chrome, Safari, Firefox, Finder, Xcode, VS Code, Slack and Zoom as of 2026-09 and remains valid for existing users without being offered as an alternative. `HOTKEY_PRESETS` retains historical constants for compatibility tests; the menu offers only `DEFAULT_HOTKEY`, not this list. Custom values pass `validateAccelerator`: at least CommandOrControl or Control, optional Alt/Shift, one supported key, no duplicate modifiers, at most 64 characters, and a short macOS reserved list. Canonical order is CommandOrControl, Control, Alt, Shift, key. Unsupported stored values still fall back with a warning.

Custom… records physical letter/digit/function/punctuation key codes and named Space/arrows. Unsupported codes, including numpad keys, are refused. Shifted punctuation aliases normalize to Shift plus the base key before reserved-combination validation. The shortcut group is the only value-carrying control; main revalidates and canonicalizes its candidate before saving. Escape, Tab/Shift+Tab navigation away, clicking elsewhere, window blur/close, renderer failure and a 15-second timeout cancel capture. Main suspends the OS registration before acknowledging arming, then restores it on completion or cancellation; quit disposes it. Each capture is a lease owned by the window that armed it, separate from the setting transaction it may start. Cancel, blur, timeout, close, renderer failure, a recording lock and quit release it at once — even while a confirmed save is still writing — and restore the registrations committed at that moment. Release is idempotent; a late event from an older window, or an older save completing, ends only its own lease and never a newer window's capture. A confirmed save runs to completion after its window closes or crashes; an unconfirmed draft is never submitted. `AppShortcuts` owns both registrations and the persist-then-register order below. Recording locks the group. Invalid candidates show a localized reason and leave settings unchanged. Off preserves a custom accelerator as a selectable option. The capture button is keyboard reachable and announces its state with `aria-live`.

`pnpm acceptance` supports letters/digits, Space, arrows, F1–F20 and common unshifted punctuation using macOS key codes. Other combinations fail explicitly with the accelerator in the error before recording begins. Non-US keyboard behavior requires native verification; physical `event.code` does not establish Electron layout compatibility.

A registration the OS refuses (another app owns the combination, or `register` throws) is never silent: it is logged as `hotkey: registration failed for …`, shown in the menu label as "Shortcut unavailable (in use by another app): …", and announced by a notification. The setting is still saved so the user's choice survives a relaunch; the tray keeps working. Disabling the shortcut leaves tray behavior unchanged and remembers the accelerator so re-enabling restores it. Changing the shortcut persists first and registers second: a failed write keeps the old registration and reports "Could not save the shortcut". If a recording starts while that write is pending, the registration change is deferred (`request` → `flush` on the next settled state) so the combination that started the session can still stop it; until then the menu shows the saved choice as unavailable.

`pnpm acceptance:shortcut` verifies reported registration failure and failed-choice persistence across isolated Electron processes, including process/window/shortcut/temporary-data cleanup. Notification assertions observe invocation, not macOS banner delivery; this does not guarantee detection of every case where another app intercepts keys. Plan 020 closed with maintainer acceptance of this evidence, retaining actual OS conflict and notification banners as untested limitations; see the [closure record](../verification/history-2026-09.md#plan-020-closure--2026-09-23).

## Language

Source: [i18n.ts](../../src/shared/i18n.ts). English (`en`) is the default, including upgrades from settings files without a language field. The user can choose English or Traditional Chinese (`zh-TW`) from Settings → General → Language. There is no implicit OS-locale selection.

English source messages are typed catalog keys; ZH_TW supplies each translation. Translate selects a template and substitutes named placeholders; the compiler requires a value for every placeholder. The model receives language through TrayContext. Notifications read the current context when created; existing OS notifications are not rewritten retroactively.

A language action serializes a settings save, switches the in-memory language only after success, and refreshes the tray. Failure preserves the old language and reports the failure in that language. Switching during recording changes presentation only; recording state, source, output location, and quality snapshot are unaffected.

Menu labels, tooltips, permission guidance, folder dialog title, application error summaries, and notifications are localized. Diagnostic details and developer tool output remain English. Technical failure details remain in the log rather than appearing as untranslated fragments in Chinese error notifications. Native OS permission dialogs follow macOS language settings, not the app's selector.

## Screen permission

Source: [permission.ts](../../src/main/permission.ts). PermissionWatcher is created only on macOS.

1. Check getMediaAccessStatus('screen') every five seconds and on activate. A windowless app cannot rely on activate alone. This stage never prompts and materialises nothing, so polling it is cheap.
2. If not granted, clear validation and any pending retry, emit needsPermission, and call getSources at most once per process to register the app/show the system prompt.
3. If granted but unvalidated, require at least one capturable screen. A validation still unanswered after four seconds shows relaunch guidance (needsRelaunch), but the call keeps its slot: the deadline cannot cancel getSources.
4. At most one watcher-owned getSources call, prompt or validation, is unresolved at a time. While it is pending only stage 1 is polled. A grant that arrives during the prompt is validated once the prompt settles; a prompt result never counts as validation.
5. Cache success. A failure requires relaunch and is retried after a backoff counted from that failure's completion: 5, 10, 20 and 40 seconds, then every 60 seconds. Revocation resets the backoff.
6. Revocation, an actual permission-denied capture (markRelaunchRequired) and stop() start a new generation: a validation from an older generation that settles later is logged and ignored, and a fresh one follows. stop() removes the interval, the activate listener and both timers; a call still pending stays owned until it settles.

Only stage 1 is polled, and caching stage 2 for the process is what makes that safe. Cap polled the equivalent macOS call for permission state — `SCShareableContent`, which materialises every window, app and display — for the whole process lifetime, leaking about 15 MB/min until macOS exhausted swap ([CapSoftware/Cap issue #2023](https://github.com/CapSoftware/Cap/issues/2023), fixed in 0.5.9 as "Memory growth while idle on macOS"). Their post-incident design is the one used here: a cheap preflight gate polled freely, an expensive validation cached after one success, serialized, and retried no sooner than the poll period after it completes, with the same five-second and four-second constants. Cap also times out `ShareableContent::current`, but dropping that future was not shown to cancel the macOS request, so RecordStuff keeps the real promise owned instead of issuing a replacement (plan 027). A call that never returns cannot be recovered inside the process: the guidance is to relaunch, which ends it with the process. A runtime revocation is caught by stage 1, by the capture attempt itself, and in practice by macOS asking the app to relaunch. A revocation and regrant that both fall between two polls are not observed, so a validation started before them can still apply.

Only changed permission states are emitted. Recorder always stores the latest status, including while starting, recording or stopping, and never interrupts a running session for it. Every return to a non-busy state (saved, capture failure or start failure) settles from that stored status into idle or needsPermission, so a revocation reported once during a session is not lost, and a grant later in the same session needs no further event. needsPermission keeps lastSavedPath, so Show last recording stays in the menu; the recorder restores the other idle details (outputDirUnavailable, unless the folder was changed meanwhile) once permission returns. The saved banner still yields to permission guidance, as described under notifications. Actual track termination, capture failure, or an OS-requested quit follows the recording cleanup path.

System Settings opens through a fixed ScreenCapture URL. The app does not reset TCC. The missing-permission menu always offers relaunch because the running process may not observe a new grant. System audio has its own permission and is validated through returned audio tracks. See the [verification record](../verification/README.md) for first-grant, denial, recovery, and revocation evidence.

## Settings and output folder

Sources: [settings.ts](../../src/main/settings.ts), [quality.ts](../../src/shared/quality.ts).

```json
{
  "version": 3,
  "outputDir": "/Users/example/Movies/RecordStuff",
  "quality": { "videoQuality": "standard", "resolutionCap": "source", "frameRate": 30 },
  "language": "en",
  "hotkey": { "enabled": true, "accelerator": "CommandOrControl+Shift+1" },
  "notifications": true
}
```

OutputDir must be a nonempty absolute path. Version 1 loads with default quality, version 1 and 2 load with the default shortcut (each logged as a warning), and both are written as version 3 on the next save. The additive language field is optional on disk for older files; it defaults to English. Unsupported language values default to English with a warning, preserving valid folder/quality settings. `hotkey.accelerator` must pass the shared accelerator validator; a missing or unsupported hotkey block in a v3 file falls back to the default shortcut with a warning and keeps the other fields. Unsupported versions or invalid folder data fall back to defaults; an invalid quality block alone does not discard a valid folder. Obsolete audioQuality fields are ignored. `notifications` is additive like `updates`: a missing or non-boolean value reads as `true` without a warning, because a file written before the switch existed is not a broken file.

Save operations are serialized and derive each update from the last committed settings. Write and fsync settings.json.tmp, rename it, then update memory. A failed save removes the temporary file and rejects its caller without blocking later saves. Flushing before the rename prevents an empty or half-written settings.json after a crash or power loss; without a directory fsync, a very recent change may still revert to the previous file.

Folder selection uses a native dialog, with app focus on macOS. A successful save clears the idle folder-unavailable flag and refreshes labels. Actual writability is probed when recording starts. There is no automatic fallback to a different output folder.

Quality and the shortcut can change only while idle/needsPermission, and each recording has a quality snapshot. Platform frame-rate clamping affects effective settings, not persisted preferences.

## Logs

Source: [log.ts](../../src/main/log.ts). Current macOS paths are `~/Library/Logs/recordstuff/recordstuff.log` and `~/Library/Application Support/recordstuff/settings.json`. Electron's internal lowercase app name determines these paths; the displayed product name remains RecordStuff.

Each line starts with a UTC ISO timestamp. Startup records app/Electron/OS versions, the launch's run id, output folder, quality, packaged status, and executable path. Recording events include session, state, capture report, first chunk, saved (with any early-stop reason), failed, the stall and low-disk warnings with the writer backlog, a start failure reclassified to a retained disk error, and interruption sentinels found at launch. `power: suspend` and `power: resume` lines name the in-flight session and state. Logs include local paths, but no media bytes.

Session records (plan 029). A run id, launch time plus pid such as `20260925T101530123Z-4242`, appears in the `start:` line only; ordinary lines carry no prefix. Beside the human-readable capture, `saved` and `failed:` lines the app writes one versioned record per event, `session-record: {"v":1,"run":…,"kind":…}`: `capture` (session, requested quality, capture report), `saved` (session, final path, recording and stop-request times, early-stop reason), `failed` (session, code, detail, file outcome, kept and temporary paths, times; written even when no file remains) and `refused` (a preflight refusal, which names no session). JSON keeps paths with spaces, quotes or line breaks on one escaped line. The Recorder carries the session on its terminal events because a failure's cleanup can finish after the next session started, so completion order is no identity. Development analyzers pair recordings by run and session id and read only these records where a launch writes them, never both forms as two outcomes; older logs are not rewritten.

All messages go to stdout. Before an append, a file larger than 5 MiB rotates through three archives; development runners follow a rotation with file-identity cursors ([tooling](tooling.md#selecting-acceptance-coverage)) and the policy itself is unchanged. Synchronous writes provide immediate diagnostics for the windowless app. A disk logging failure reports once to stderr and disables file output for the process while retaining stdout. Uncaught main errors also show a localized dialog; unhandled rejections are logged.

Show log reveals the file, falling back to opening its directory if absent. It does not affect capture. Development Electron.app and installed RecordStuff.app are different permission subjects; inspect executable before interpreting a grant or failure.

## Official website

The website ([website/](../../website/), see [tooling](tooling.md#official-website)) is English-only by maintainer decision; the app UI and repository guides stay bilingual, and the footer links the Traditional Chinese installation guide. Three structures were built on one content layer and compared locally on 2026-09-20: A, T3 Code's page set (home with hero, illustration and feature grid; separate Download, Help and Support); B, a single scrolling page with collapsible help; C, a download-first home with one combined help page. The maintainer chose A for its familiarity and deep-linkable help; B and C were deleted. A second round compared three visual directions on that structure (Ember: campsite night, orange accent; Paper: light monochrome; Aurora: gradient glow); the maintainer chose Ember with a geometric sans (Geist) instead of a serif, no eyebrow labels or section leads, the real ring-and-dot mark instead of the squared app icon, and a hero that animates one click starting a recording. A fourth round replaced the orange-and-purple palette with graphite neutrals (white type, grey second headline line, red only for the recording dot, no coloured glows) and added a camera push-in toward the menu-bar icon on each click of the story. Three landscape styles (Dusk realistic, Wire line art, Facet low-poly), all re-tinted to the neutral palette with fire as the only warm note, were compared at `/preview/`; the maintainer chose Facet and the others were deleted. The camera now anchors at the screen's top-right corner so the whole corner block enlarges together and the icon stays inside the thicker menu bar, the loop was slowed to fourteen seconds with 1.4-second eased push-ins and pull-backs, and the recording state was corrected to what the tray really shows: a filled dot with the title "REC" beside it, not a red dot inside the ring. A fifth pass raised the base type size to 18 px (every size is in rem, so the whole scale grew inside the same column widths; long paths in the help tables now wrap), enlarged the callout text and arrow and tilted the sentence from 3° to 9° so it follows the arrow's sweep and dropped it 14 units to leave air under the arrow's tail, and anchored the page vignette to the viewport instead of the document, because sizing it to the page made a short page (Support) open lighter than a long one (Help). A final pass added three seconds of stillness before the story starts, replaced the second push-in with a callout arrow ("Click again to stop recording"), made the notification pop in instantly, moved the idle icon next to the battery so the item visibly shifts left for REC, and centred every menu-bar element vertically. Pages: `/` (purpose, download button, features), `/download` (verified version, size, date, source commit, SHA-256 with copy, SHA256SUMS and release.json links, older versions via GitHub Releases), `/help` (install, Open Anyway, permissions, record, language, manual update, remove, retained data) and `/support` (verified platform boundary, privacy, source and issues). ## Update checks

[updates.ts](../../src/main/updates.ts) owns the update-check lifecycle, independently of recording. Settings → General contains Check for updates…, its result, and Check for updates on launch. Results never change the tray title or produce notifications/dialogs. Starting, recording and saving disable update actions; checks and pending results wait for idle/needsPermission.

Settings version 3 gains an optional `updates: { enabled, lastAttempt }` block. Older files default to enabled and no prior attempt. Atomic serialized writes preserve unrelated settings. The launch attempt timestamp is persisted before networking, including failed checks, to enforce a 24-hour limit across restarts. Future timestamps after a clock correction are treated as due. Manual checks bypass this limit and still proceed if timestamp persistence fails (with a log entry). No timer polls during the app lifetime. Disabling the preference affects launch checks only.

The app injects Electron `net.fetch` to use Chromium networking and system proxy/PAC settings; unit tests inject their own transport. A check reads `https://record.ericts.com/release.json`, falling back to the repository's GitHub latest-release API. Each source has an 8-second deadline and shutdown aborts pending requests. Stable semantic versions are compared with `app.getVersion()`; incompatible assets, malformed data and prereleases cannot produce an update offer. A failed launch check restores the previous menu and logs; a failed manual check offers Releases and a retry. Successful checks display the local check time or a newer version that opens the fixed website download page. Downloads and replacement stay manual.

## Notification switch

Settings → General contains a Notifications On/Off switch with, on macOS, an Open notification settings… button in the same card, so the switch and the OS permission that can override it read as one decision rather than two unrelated settings. `AppTray.show` checks the switch before anything else and logs `notification: turned off in settings, dropped: …`, so one boolean governs every notification the app sends — saved recordings, errors, and every write-failure hint. The switch is locked while a capture is running, like every other preference.

The app deliberately does not mirror the OS notification permission. Electron exposes no way to read it: `systemPreferences.getMediaAccessStatus` accepts `microphone`, `camera` and `screen` only, and `Electron Framework` contains no `getNotificationSettingsWithCompletionHandler`. It does contain `requestAuthorizationWithOptions:completionHandler:`, so Electron asks macOS for authorization from inside `Notification.show()` — the first notification of a fresh install is what raises the system prompt. `UNUserNotificationCenter` offers that prompt only while the status is `notDetermined`, and the status is kept per bundle ID and survives reinstalling the app, so there is exactly one chance to show it and a denial is final as far as the app is concerned. It is therefore spent on first launch, by the first-run hint that also tells the user where a menu-bar app lives: launch is when macOS is already asking this app for screen recording, so the notification ask arrives in that same context instead of at the end of the user's first recording. The switch stays on by default — a recorder that never says it saved reads as broken, and an unspent prompt the user never finds is no better than a denied one. Turning the switch off and on again also sends a confirmation notification, which doubles as a delivery test the user can repeat. A status line would have to claim a permission state the app cannot read, so the group's note names the recovery path instead — System Settings → Notifications → RecordStuff — and the macOS action opens that pane. Turning the switch off suppresses OS notifications. Recording failures still appear in the menu bar and the Recording failures section described below.

This follows Cap's design (`apps/desktop/src-tauri/src/notifications.rs`), where the send path checks one `enable_notifications` boolean and nothing else. Cap additionally gates its switch on `isPermissionGranted()`, which `@tauri-apps/plugin-notification` provides and Electron does not; the note text carries that gap instead. Plan 019 first implemented a Node-API bridge to macOS `UserNotifications` to close it. That was reverted: the bridge made a bridge-load failure — a wrong-architecture or too-new `.node` — silently suppress every notification, including the implicit authorization request Electron would otherwise have made, which is strictly worse than having no status at all. The bridge is preserved on the `wip/019-native-notification-bridge` branch.

The static website endpoint projects the online-verified release manifest with version, tag, platform/architecture, DMG metadata, publication date and trusted page URLs. Unverified preview builds emit an error object instead of an advertised release. Vercel caches the feed for five minutes; the app requests it without caching. `pnpm site:check` verifies the built feed matches the manifest; the deployment workflow also runs `check-release.mts --dir .vercel/output/static` against the actual Vercel output. No installation identifier, query parameter, telemetry, updater dependency or changed signing identity is involved.

The macOS settings action opens the Notifications overview; select RecordStuff there to reach its permission controls. This existing route was retained when plan 019 closed. Ordinary settings value/text updates preserve the live form controls, focus and scroll position; only tab/structure changes rebuild the form. Save-only interaction locks retain normal opacity to avoid a brief whole-panel dimming, while recording/platform restrictions remain visibly disabled.

## Settings shortcut

`CommandOrControl+Alt+,` (⌘⌥, on macOS) opens, restores and focuses the single Settings panel through `openSettings`, including while recording. It never toggles recording; ordinary ⌘, remains untouched. `settings-hotkey.ts` owns this registration independently of the recording shortcut and releases only its own key on quit. The tray advertises it only when registered and otherwise explains failures in the selected language.

New recording choices cannot use the platform-equivalent Settings combination. A legacy stored collision is preserved and takes priority; change or disable the recording shortcut through tray Settings to recover. Both registrations suspend during custom capture and resume when its lease is released (commit, cancel, blur, timeout, close or renderer failure), without waiting for a pending save. Recording locks still apply.

### Screen preference and diagnostics

Settings → Recording → Screen lists Primary display followed by unique connected displays from `screen.getAllDisplays()`; opening the panel never enumerates capture sources or asks for capture permission. The default follows the current primary. A specific choice stores only id and label in the existing version-3 settings file; versions 1–3 without the field use the default, and invalid values warn and fall back. Empty names receive localized id-based labels. The panel sends a choice id and main resolves the current action, including its label.

Added/removed/metrics events always advance topology generation; settled UI refreshes immediately and busy UI refreshes when settled. Recording locks selection. An absent or ambiguous stored choice remains selected but disabled, alongside Primary and available recovery choices. The idle tray names a healthy explicit target. Current absence and the last display failure are distinguished in both projections. Last failure lives in memory even when notifications are off; only successful recording start or a successfully saved different choice clears it. Failed saves preserve both preference and diagnostic and send the normal preference-gated notification. There is no tray submenu or system picker; system audio policy is unchanged.

Appearance in General offers System default (the default), Light and Dark, including while recording. After a successful save main sets `nativeTheme.themeSource`, updating native window appearance and CSS immediately, and restores it at startup. Missing or invalid values in older files fall back to system independently of other preferences.

## Recording failure results

Recorder emits a separate failure-status update before file cleanup completes. Main retains individual failures and their acknowledgement in `userData/recording-history.json` (version 2), independent of notification preferences and capture state. New failures appear first; delayed cleanup updates its own ID without replacing or reordering other failures. All unread records and the 20 most recently acknowledged records are retained. This is failure history, not a media library or orphan-recovery mechanism.

There is one tray item. Its idle ring carries an integrated warning badge whenever any failure is unread; recording still takes precedence with the filled icon and REC. The menu reports the unread count and offers View recording failures. Left click still starts/stops recording. Error notifications open the same Settings history, focusing the newest unread failure (or newest record when all are reviewed), without acknowledging it. Opening a menu, dismissing a notification, successful recordings and elapsed time do not clear failures.

Recording failures sits above preferences in either Settings tab. Each independently expandable record shows its time, reason, file outcome, recovery guidance and technical details. Pending cleanup disables Got it and has no reveal action. Partial files are checked before presentation and again before reveal; unknown or missing paths remain hints, not promises of playable or recoverable media. File/folder/permission actions retain recording locks. Normal updates preserve each row's expansion and identity, so an older button cannot act on a newer record.

Got it durably acknowledges only the offered settled ID and collapses that row. The badge clears only after every retained failure is acknowledged. Reviewed rows can be removed from history; neither manual removal nor trimming the oldest reviewed rows deletes recordings or logs. Acknowledgement and removal become visible only once the snapshot containing them is durable; meanwhile the row shows a localized saving line, its buttons stay focusable with `aria-disabled` and ignore activation, and other rows, preferences and recording controls stay usable. Failed acknowledgement/removal leaves the previous visible state intact with a per-row error. Retry saving reminder independently saves the history without changing acknowledgement, including for unread or pending failures. Saving any update writes the current history snapshot, so a successful retry also persists other unsaved failures.

Focus follows the action's recorded intent, not whether `document.activeElement` survived the wait: after acknowledgement, collapse or failure it returns to that row's summary, after removing the last row to the active tab. It never moves when the user moved focus or the window lost focus during the wait, and never enters another row's controls. Result actions bypass the preference save queue and never end shortcut capture.

Restart does not replay notifications. Interrupted pending cleanup becomes unknown. A session that was still recording when an earlier process ended (force-quit, crash, process kill or power loss) is reported once at launch through its interruption sentinel as an unread `app_terminated` entry: "RecordStuff did not exit normally while recording.", timed at the session's start, with guidance that the file may be incomplete and is not repaired. Its temporary path is rechecked like a previously confirmed partial below, so it offers reveal only while a nonempty file exists there and is otherwise unknown with the path as a hint. It raises no notification, and it does not reappear after acknowledgement or another restart because the sentinel is removed once the entry is saved. No recovery, remux or repair is performed. Previously confirmed partial files start unknown and are rechecked sequentially with a two-second bound; a timeout stops further checks to avoid filling the filesystem worker pool; a successful check restores partial status while preserving intervening acknowledgement. Unavailable paths retain their candidate/provenance for another restart. Existing bytes alone never promote interrupted cleanup to confirmed preservation. Removed IDs cannot be resurrected by a delayed check or terminal event.

On first use, a valid `recording-result.json` v1 record migrates into the separate history file; the legacy file is preserved. A valid empty history suppresses re-import. A corrupt, oversized or newer history file is logged and protected from overwrite; subsequent writes report a persistence warning. The serialized history has a 32 MiB safety bound and validated unique IDs; unread records are not evicted to meet that bound, so exceeding it reports a save failure. This is not unlimited durable storage.

History uses a private temporary file, fsync and rename through Node's asynchronous `fs.promises` (the libuv threadpool); main never waits on the disk. Startup reads the file asynchronously, never writes before it has loaded, and merges failures that arrive meanwhile by ID; Settings shows a localized loading line until then. One owner keeps at most one write in flight and one coalesced follow-up; each snapshot is the whole history, so it carries every failure and action outcome of an earlier one, and a completion settles only the revision it contained. An older completion never clears the warning of a newer unsaved change. Unchanged history is not rewritten after startup checks.

Save failure keeps new information in memory and shows a localized warning only for records that differ from the last saved snapshot. Ordinary I/O failures retry automatically on one timer, 2, 5, 15 and then every 30 seconds while unsaved; success resets the backoff, a clean history stops it, and a failure streak logs once plus its recovery. Manual retry starts promptly and joins an active write instead of adding a second writer. Unreadable or newer history is never retried or overwritten and says so instead of suggesting disk space; a history over the 32 MiB bound asks for reviewed rows to be removed. Atomic replacement cannot guarantee recovery from power loss or a full/unwritable disk, and background saving does not add disk throughput. Restored permission errors use historical guidance and offer Relaunch only when current permission state requires it. Failure IDs are UUIDs across processes.

Serialization stays on main without a worker. Whole-snapshot `JSON.stringify` at the 32 MiB bound measured about 40 ms on an M1 Pro, over one 16 ms frame, but handing that snapshot to a worker costs a comparable structured clone on main, so records instead cache their encoded JSON and fingerprints (records are immutable) and history loops yield after about 8 ms of work. Measured longest single main-thread turn: saves at most about 10 ms, including at the bound; startup reading takes one turn of about 38 ms at the bound (a single `JSON.parse`), about 6 ms at 3.8 MiB and about 2 ms for a typical history. See the [verification record](../verification/history-2026-09.md#plan-036-closure--2026-09-25).

## Deferred quit

Normal quit waits for recording and all pending disk work, even when the tray is idle. After 13 seconds with work pending, an English/Traditional Chinese information dialog says RecordStuff will remain open and asks the user to retry after saving/cleanup finishes. Repeated requests cannot bypass this gate. Starting capture retains stop intent across deferral. Relaunch is registered only after safe admission and is canceled by a deferred quit. No force-exit control is offered for media work.

Only after media is safe, while new captures stay refused, quit and relaunch attempt the latest failure-history save for up to 5 seconds (a local save measured about 5–10 ms; the 32 MiB bound under 200 ms). Saved history exits normally. If a write is still in flight, a warning offers Keep waiting / Stay in app and never an exit, because a timed-out write is not proof that the OS stopped writing. If the save failed with no write in flight, the warning lists the number of unsaved reminders with their times and reasons (five, then a count) and offers Retry, Stay in app and a separate Exit without saving these reminders; that choice is logged and closes the history writer. Staying reopens capture admission and automatic retry and clears relaunch intent. Repeated requests join the running attempt and bring its prompt forward. Nothing is acknowledged or saved as a side effect of exit, and no OS notification is replayed. Force-quit and power loss may lose reminders that were not yet saved.
